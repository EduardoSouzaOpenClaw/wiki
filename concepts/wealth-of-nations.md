# The Wealth of Nations

*An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations* (1776) by [[adam-smith]], the founding text of modern economics.

## Overview

Published in 1776, *The Wealth of Nations* systematized economic thought and introduced concepts like the division of labor, free trade, and the "invisible hand." It argued that national wealth comes from productive labor and trade, not from accumulated gold as mercantilists claimed.

## Key Themes

- **Critique of mercantilism:** Gold and silver are not wealth — they are merely medium of exchange. Real wealth is what goods money can buy.
- **Division of labor:** Specialization increases productivity (the famous pin factory example)
- **Invisible hand:** Self-interested actors inadvertently serve societal interests
- **Free trade:** Restrictions on trade reduce collective prosperity
- **Role of government:** Limited to defense, justice, and public works

## Famous Quote

> "No society can be prosperous and happy if most of its members are poor and miserable." — Book I, Chapter 8, "Of the Wages of Labour"
