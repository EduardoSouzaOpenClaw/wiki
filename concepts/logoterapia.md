A form of psychotherapy developed by Viktor Frankl, based on the premise that the primary human motivation is a "will to meaning" — not pleasure (Freud) or power (Adler). When this meaning is frustrated, existential frustration occurs, leading to noogenic neurosis.

## Core Concepts

1. **Will to Meaning:** The drive to find meaningful purpose in life
2. **Existential Vacuum:** A sense of meaninglessness, especially in modern societies
3. **Life Questions vs. Death Questions:** Frankl distinguished between problems (answerable) and paradoxes (must be lived)
4. **The Unconscious God:** The spiritual unconscious, capable of reaching for meaning even when the conscious mind cannot articulate it

## Techniques

- **Dereflection:** Shifting attention away from self-obsession toward meaning
- **Socratic dialogue:** Helping patients discover their unique meaning
- **Attitudinal values:** Choosing one's response to unavoidable suffering
- **矛盾意向 (Paradoxical Intention):** Willing the feared outcome to defuse anxiety
