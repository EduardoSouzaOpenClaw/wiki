# Invisible Hand

[[adam-smith]]'s metaphor for how individual self-interest can spontaneously produce societal benefit without central coordination.

## Overview

The "invisible hand" describes the emergent social good that arises when individuals pursue their own economic interests in a competitive marketplace. It is Smith's core argument for why free markets work.

## Key Points

- Individuals pursuing self-interest inadvertently benefit society
- No central planner needed — prices and competition coordinate
- Self-interest drives innovation and efficiency
- Trade is mutually beneficial: "not from the benevolence of the butcher...but from their regard to their own interest"

## Context and Nuance

Smith did not advocate pure selfishness or unregulated markets. He also wrote *The Theory of Moral Sentiments* on sympathy and ethics, and criticized rent-seeking behavior. The invisible hand is a theoretical mechanism, not a moral endorsement of greed.
