The relationship between human suffering and the search for meaning. Central to logotherapy: when suffering is given meaning, it becomes bearable and can even be transformative.

## Core Thesis (Frankl)

Pain is unavoidable. But **suffering is optional** — it depends on whether the pain has meaning. When a person can assign meaning to their suffering, the suffering itself loses its destructive power and can become a source of growth.

## Mechanisms

- **Meaning-making:** Finding a "why" that gives purpose to the "what"
- **Attitudinal values:** When physical pain cannot be avoided, one can still choose inner composure (the "last of human freedoms" — choosing one's attitude)
- **Transcendence:** Connecting personal suffering to something larger (love for others, creative work, acceptance of fate)

## Applications

- Therapeutic contexts (logotherapy)
- Crisis and resilience (finding purpose in hardship)
- Existential questions about mortality, loss, failure
