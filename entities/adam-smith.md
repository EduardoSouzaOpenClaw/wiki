# Adam Smith

Scottish philosopher and economist (1723–1790), widely considered the father of modern economics and classical liberalism. Author of *The Theory of Moral Sentiments* (1759) and *An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations* (1776).

## Key Facts

- **Born:** 1723 in Kirkcaldy, Scotland
- **Died:** 1790 in Edinburgh, Scotland
- **Academic career:** Professor of moral philosophy at University of Glasgow
- **Influences:** Enlightenment thought, Scottish Enlightenment
- **Key concept:** "Invisible hand" — individuals pursuing self-interest can produce societal benefit
- **Major works:**
  - *The Theory of Moral Sentiments* (1759) — on moral philosophy and sympathy
  - *An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations* (1776) — founding text of economics

## Core Ideas

- **Against "utility of poverty":** Smith rejected the idea that low wages prevent laziness and overpopulation. Higher wages are a natural consequence of economic growth, not a problem to avoid.
- **Wealth of a nation:** Not measured by gold in royal coffers, but by the standard of living of the common people. "If the people suffer, the nation is poor even if leaders have money."
- **Justice for workers:** Workers deserve a fair share of what they produce — for food, clothing, and shelter.
- **Critique of mercantilism:** Advocated individual liberty and morality against mercantile (merchant and state) control of the economy.
- **Invisible hand:** Self-interested individual actions can collectively benefit society.

## Famous Quotations

> "No society can be prosperous and happy if most of its members are poor and miserable." — *The Wealth of Nations*, Book I, Chapter 8

> "Everything for us and nothing for others seems to have been, in all ages of the world, the vile maxim of the masters of humanity."

> "It is not from the benevolence of the butcher, the brewer, or the baker that we expect our dinner, but from their regard to their own interest."

> "Wealth does not consist in money, nor in gold and silver, but in what money buys."

## Legacy

Considered the father of modern economics and classical liberalism. His ideas on free trade and the invisible hand influenced David Ricardo, Karl Marx, and generations of economists. Despite being reduced by some to a defender of selfishness or savage capitalism, Smith maintained that economic prosperity only has legitimacy if it results in a fairer, happier society for the majority.
